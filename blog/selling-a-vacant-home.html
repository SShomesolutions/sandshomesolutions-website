Selling a vacant home: why every month you wait costs you more.

An empty house can feel like a low stakes, no rush situation. It isn't. Here's what it's actually costing you every month it sits there.

There's a strange kind of calm that comes with an empty house, no tenants calling, no daily wear and tear, nothing urgent demanding your attention. That calm is deceptive. A vacant home isn't a neutral, cost free situation, it's actively costing you money and risk every single month, just more quietly than a house with problems in it.

The costs that don't stop just because no one's living there

  • Property taxes, which keep accruing whether the home is occupied or not
  • Insurance, and often at a higher rate, since many standard homeowner policies either exclude or limit coverage on vacant properties, sometimes requiring a separate, more expensive vacant home policy
  • Utilities, at minimum enough to keep pipes from freezing and prevent humidity damage
  • Basic maintenance, lawn care, snow removal, general upkeep, all of which someone still has to handle even with no one living there
  • Mortgage payments, if there's still a loan on the property, which don't pause just because it's empty

None of these costs are dramatic on their own in any single month. Added together, month after month, they quietly become a significant, ongoing drain with nothing coming in to offset it.

The risks that grow the longer a home sits empty

Insurance complications

Beyond simply costing more, vacant home insurance often exists precisely because standard policies get nervous about empty properties. Insurers know that vacant homes see slower response times to problems, a small leak can become significant water damage before anyone notices, since no one's there to catch it early.

Deterioration nobody catches in time

Homes are, in a sense, meant to be lived in. Without regular use, small issues compound. A minor roof leak becomes a bigger one. Pests move in undisturbed. HVAC systems that don't run regularly can develop problems that go unnoticed for months. Every month of vacancy is a month where a small, cheap fix could quietly become a much larger one before anyone's there to catch it.

Target risk

Vacant properties are, unfortunately, more visible targets for vandalism, break-ins, or squatting than occupied homes, particularly if the vacancy becomes obvious over time, an overgrown lawn, accumulated mail, no lights ever on.

If you inherited a vacant property

This situation comes up often with inherited homes specifically, where the property sits empty during probate or while family sorts out next steps. If that's your situation, you're not alone in feeling like there's no urgency, take a look at our guide on inherited property for more on that specific situation.

Your real options

List it and wait out a traditional sale

Possible, but the vacancy costs and risks keep accumulating during the weeks or months a traditional listing can take, especially if the home needs any updates first to be market ready.

Rent it out

Solves the vacancy problem, but trades it for landlord responsibilities you may not want, especially if the property has sat empty specifically because you don't want that role.

Sell directly, as it sits

We buy vacant properties in their current condition, closing quickly enough to stop the monthly drain instead of letting it continue while a traditional sale plays out.
