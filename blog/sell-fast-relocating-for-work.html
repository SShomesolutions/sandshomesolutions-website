How to sell your home fast when you're relocating for work.

A job relocation rarely comes with a generous timeline. Here's how to sell quickly without leaving money or peace of mind behind.

A relocation offer is exciting and stressful at the same time. There's a new role, sometimes a hard start date, and often a company that wants you settled in before you've even had time to think through what happens to the house you're leaving behind. If you're in this situation right now, here's an honest look at your real options and the tradeoffs of each.

Why a traditional sale is risky on a tight timeline

A typical traditional sale takes weeks just to get to an accepted offer, and then another 30 to 45 days to close once a buyer is under contract, longer if their financing runs into any snags. If your new job starts in three weeks, that math simply doesn't work. You're left choosing between delaying a start date, which isn't always possible, or moving before the house is sold and managing everything remotely from a new city and time zone.

Managing a sale from far away adds its own problems. You can't be there for last minute showings, you can't quickly handle a repair request that comes up during inspection, and coordinating with an agent, a title company, and a buyer all from a distance adds friction to every single step.

What relocation packages actually cover, and what they don't

If your employer is offering any kind of relocation assistance, it's worth understanding exactly what's included before assuming it solves this problem for you. Some packages include a guaranteed buyout of your home at an appraised value, which genuinely solves the timeline problem. Many others only cover moving costs, temporary housing, or a lump sum, and leave the actual sale of your house entirely up to you. Read the specifics of your offer closely, don't assume "relocation package" automatically means your home sale is handled.

Your real options

List traditionally and manage it remotely

Possible, but genuinely difficult. You'll likely need a trusted local contact, family, a friend, or a property manager, to handle showings and any issues that come up while you're gone. This can work, but it adds real logistical weight during a period that's already stressful.

Rent it out instead of selling

An option if you're not in a rush to fully sever ties, but it means becoming a long distance landlord, with everything that involves, tenant screening, maintenance calls, and ongoing management, on top of settling into a new city and job.

Sell directly to a cash buyer before you go

We can typically close in two to three weeks, often before your relocation date, meaning you leave with the sale fully behind you instead of managing it from a different state. No repairs, no staging for showings you can't attend, no remote negotiation.

Being honest about the tradeoff

A direct sale won't net you the same number as a fully marketed traditional listing might, if that listing had the luxury of time and your presence to manage it closely. What it does offer is genuine closure before you go, no lingering house to worry about while you're trying to focus on a new job and a new city. For a lot of people in the middle of a relocation, that's worth more than squeezing out the last few percentage points of sale price.
